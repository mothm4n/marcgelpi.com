## Start with a problem your manager can see

Start with a problem your manager needs to solve. Ask for one OKR cycle with a deadline and a review.

People may face conflicting requests without knowing which task can wait.

Look for signs you can show:

{{< okrs-observable-costs >}}

Choose two or three examples and explain their cost. A crowded plan, a missed date or a repeated request for help gives your manager something concrete to discuss.

## Explain what OKRs mean

OKR means Objectives and Key Results.

An objective describes the result you want. A key result sets a measurable target and a deadline.

In the example below, the objective is to reduce avoidable delays when new customers start using the service. The key result sets a target for reducing the share of customers who miss their agreed start date. Include customers who are still waiting after that date.

The goal helps the teams choose their work. The measure shows whether the customer result improves. A regular meeting gives the teams a place to review progress and make decisions.

Keep the measures you already use for finances and daily work. Use OKRs for the few results that need particular attention now.

## Ask for one OKR cycle

Ask your manager to approve one cycle. Choose one problem and a few teams.

Choose a period that gives you time to observe the result. This example uses three months. Some results need longer.

Name who will run the test and who can decide which work comes first. Set a review date. Agree which existing meeting or report the test will replace.

Before you start, agree how you will check both the result and the way the teams work. Reaching a target is one part of the review. You also need to know whether the cycle helped the teams make useful decisions without adding too much work.
