If AI helps a team test an idea today, waiting until the end of a two-week sprint needs a reason.

**Why it matters:** Agility depends on discovering quickly where our assumptions fail. When making a first version gets cheaper, waiting for validation, a decision, or access to users can occupy a larger share of the time from idea to feedback.

**What the evidence says:** Across three randomized field experiments involving **4,867 developers**, access to a coding assistant increased completed tasks by an estimated **26%** in the combined analysis. That measures development tasks, rather than time to customer feedback. [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-effects-of-generative-ai-on-high-skilled-work-evidence-from-three-field-experiments-with-software-developers/)

[DORA's 2025 research](https://dora.dev/research/2025/dora-report/) describes AI as an amplifier of an organisation's existing strengths and weaknesses. The benefits depend on the system around the tool. Neither study establishes an ideal team cadence.

**Let events trigger the next step:** Test a prototype when it is ready. Act when new user feedback changes a decision. Reflect on a problem while the context is fresh.

That is the shift towards **event-driven ways of working**. A sprint can still give people shared goals and regular time to step back. The [Scrum Guide](https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html) already permits delivery before a sprint ends. Teams can keep that rhythm while responding to useful evidence between scheduled conversations.

**Give that flow some discipline:**

- Control [work in progress](https://kanbanguides.org/the-kanban-guide/) through review and release. Starting more items can overload validation even when drafting is quick.
- Clarify who decides, and give them context and authority. A faster prototype still waits if ownership is unclear.
- Match [safeguards](https://sre.google/workbook/canarying-releases/) to risk. For reversible changes, use tests, gradual release, monitoring, and recovery. Bring in specialists where consequences demand deeper judgment.

Judge each practice by how it helps the team decide, learn, and protect quality. Measure the time from idea to useful feedback.
